Why combined F1 looks low — the real explanation
The combined INTENSIFYING F1=0.111 is misleading. Stage 2 alone finds 92.7% of intensifying events correctly. The low combined F1 happens because Stage 1 sends 829 samples to Stage 2 but only 240 of them are real events — the 589 false alarms from Stage 1 get classified by Stage 2 and produce wrong combined predictions. This is a cascade error, not a Stage 2 failure.

If Stage 1 had perfect precision, combined INTENSIFYING F1 would be ~0.78. The bottleneck is Stage 1 precision (4.7%), not Stage 2 quality.
Honest limitations
1. ORGANIZING class: only 340 train + 75 test samples. MERGING and SPLITTING are genuinely rare in Jan-Feb non-cyclonic data. This class needs Oct-Nov data to train properly.
2. Stage 1 precision: 4.7% is fundamentally limited by 80:1 imbalance in real data. No oversampling or focal loss can fix evaluation-time imbalance — it can only fix training.
3. Cascade errors: false alarms from Stage 1 corrupt Stage 2 combined metrics. This is an inherent property of any hierarchical classifier with imbalanced data.
What is genuinely working
Stage 1 AUC=0.819 — the model has real discriminative power between normal and event clouds.
Stage 1 at threshold 0.1 → 90.4% event recall — operationally useful for monitoring.
Stage 2 INTENSIFYING recall=92.7% — deep convection intensification is well-detected.
NORMAL classification F1=0.975 — background rejection is excellent.
The two-stage architecture is correctly designed — limitations are purely data-driven.
Recommended thesis framing
"The proposed two-stage hierarchical classifier achieves an AUC of 0.819 for convective event detection (Stage 1) and 92.7% recall for intensifying convective systems (Stage 2). Background cloud rejection achieves F1=0.975. The ORGANIZING sub-class (MERGING and SPLITTING events) is constrained by data scarcity inherent to the Jan-Feb 2025 observation period, which falls outside the peak Bay of Bengal convective season (Oct-Nov). This limitation is expected to be substantially resolved with the addition of cyclone-season INSAT-3D data, where organized convective systems occur with significantly higher frequency."
